persona: a senior content designer who writes interface copy for revenue cycle operations software used by experienced billers, collectors and managers

## Needs
- **Conventional language:** concise, standard software and RCM terms an experienced operator already knows. No coined words, metaphors or promotional claims. American spelling and the terms US payers use ("prior authorization", not "authorisation").
- **Right reader:** Vetriq screens use operator terms; client-facing screens use plain practice language for the same facts.
- **Consistent terms:** each object and state has one name used on every screen, and a term the rest of the industry uses is preferred over an internal one.
- **Distinct states:** a claim's status, an agent run's status, an approval decision, an independently verified action, and actual payment or recovery each read differently and are never merged into one another.
- **No overstatement:** investigation never reads as resolution, follow-up as payment, or a proposed correction as verified recovery.
- **Complete labels:** where relevant, text names the object, its condition, the responsible party, the next action and the consequence of not taking it.
- **No filler:** subtitles, taglines and text that only describe the interface ("This screen shows…") are removed.
- **Workflow preserved:** better words, not a different screen. The steps and what each control does stay as they are.
- **Accurate data notice:** the synthetic-data notice stays true and visible but unobtrusive.
- **Ambiguity surfaced:** where a metric or workflow rule could mean more than one thing, the copy doesn't quietly pick one. The question goes back to the product owner.

## Focus
Every screen, including any the client or its staff see. Look hardest at status labels, buttons, headings, empty states, confirmations, and any figure labelled as money recovered, collected or paid.
