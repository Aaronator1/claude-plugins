## Needs
- **A clear primary task for each role:** every screen answers one question for one person.
- **Visual hierarchy:** what is urgent, costly or blocked stands out first, with detail on demand.
- **Built for heavy daily use:** dense but readable tables, keyboard shortcuts, saved filters and sensible defaults.
- **Consistency:** the same term, status colour and component mean the same thing on every screen, in plain language rather than internal jargon.
- **Confidence in consequential actions:** a clear preview of what will happen, what can be undone and who is accountable, without friction on routine approvals.
- **Accessibility and responsiveness:** contrast, focus states, and layouts that hold up at smaller widths.

## Focus
Every screen. Look at every screenshot, not only the text.

## Extra questions
- Map the navigation and identify duplicated or competing destinations.
- Cover how each role enters the product, visual hierarchy and density, jargon, explanatory paragraphs on pages, form design on the decision screen, empty states, and consistency.
- Is there one information architecture that is clearly better than the alternatives? If there are two or three valid ones, describe each with its trade-off and say which you would lean to.
